# N1 — What the audit actually certifies (the proxy)

## 1. The certified quantity, precisely

Bouncer's gate acts on the **reward-deficit estimator**

```
Δ̂_W = r̄_L − r̄_F        (set_dueling.py:109-111)
```

where `r̄_L = mean reward on the secret Leader-C sets (run by controller C)` and
`r̄_F = mean reward on the secret Leader-F sets (run by fallback π₀)`. This is a
**measured, not counterfactual** estimate (set_dueling.py:104-106) of the
competence advantage defined in the paper (bouncer.tex:158-161):

```
Δ_W = (1/|W|) Σ_{t∈W} ( r_t^C − r_t^{π₀} ).
```

So the object the audit certifies is a **bound on the realized reward advantage
of C over π₀, in the controller's own reward metric r**, computed on a secret
random sub-sample of the controller's resource. Concretely, when the gate stays
TRUSTED it is asserting `Δ̂_W ≥ τ` (off-policy condition negated, bouncer.tex:165-168);
when `Δ̂` drops it raises GATED and floors to π₀. The safety floor lemma
(bouncer.tex:320-339, Eq. floor) then bounds *regret in r* to
`N_ep·D·r_max + αT·…`.

**Crucial framing: Δ̂ certifies an advantage in the surrogate reward r, NOT in
end utility (IPC / latency / MLP).** The certificate is exactly as good as the
fidelity of the map `r ≈ utility`. This is stated honestly in the paper's
abstract ("only as good as the competence reward it is given", bouncer.tex:69-70,
140-141) and is the load-bearing assumption A1 (bounded reward) — but A1 only
requires r∈[0,r_max], it says **nothing** about r being utility-aligned.

## 2. Assumptions the certificate rests on

From the formal setting and §6 (bouncer.tex:149-156, 307-320):

- **A1** per-decision reward r_t ∈ [0, r_max] (bounded). Gives the concentration
  bound `Var(Δ̂) ≤ (r_max²/4m)(1/n_L + 1/n_F)` (set_dueling.py:116-121).
- **A2** a "drop episode" is a window where *true* competence Δ_t < τ. Note: the
  guarantee is stated against the **true Δ in the reward metric**, not against true
  utility.
- **A3–A5** partitionable resource; secret unobservable assignment; reseed each epoch.
- **Implicit, and the binding one for N1:** `r` is a faithful proxy for the utility
  the deployment actually cares about, i.e. `sign(Δ_W^reward) = sign(Δ_W^utility)`
  and roughly monotone. **This assumption is NOT certified by Bouncer and is NOT
  testable from inside the audit** (the audit never observes utility).

## 3. What Δ̂ certifies vs. what it does NOT

**Δ̂ DOES certify (under A1–A5, w.p. ≥ 1−δN_ep):**
- A high-probability bound on the realized **reward** advantage of C over π₀ on the
  secret leaders.
- Mimicry resistance *in the reward channel*: an attacker who cannot see the
  L/F assignment cannot degrade the Follower victim's reward while keeping
  Leader-C reward clean (Prop. mimicry, bouncer.tex:369-387).
- A bounded-**regret-in-r** floor: total reward never falls more than the floor
  slack below π₀ (Lemma floor).

**Δ̂ does NOT certify:**
- Any bound on **true utility (IPC, latency, MLP, energy)**. If r under-measures a
  real benefit, Bouncer will gate a genuinely-helpful controller (false GATED);
  if r over-measures, Bouncer can stay TRUSTED while utility is harmed through a
  channel r does not see.
- Anything about C's **internal state** under online poisoning (explicitly out of
  scope, bouncer.tex:182-187).
- Faithfulness of the proxy itself. The entire certificate is *conditional* on the
  reward proxy, and that conditioning is the dominant real-world error term — far
  larger than the estimator variance the §4 chain controls.

## 4. Over-gating is a PREDICTION of the proxy, confirmed on roms

The proxy framing makes a falsifiable prediction: **when the surrogate reward
under-credits a control action that does help end utility, Bouncer will
over-gate** — i.e. it will floor to π₀ and *lose utility* even with no attacker.
This is not a bug to be apologized for; it is the proxy thesis (`certificate ⊥
utility`) becoming visible.

The ChampSim run uses an L1D **per-set cache-hit** reward as the proxy r
(champsim.json interpretation, line 53). A prefetcher's true benefit is in
latency/MLP, not in raw cache-hit rate, so the proxy systematically
under-measures a memory-bound prefetcher. Prediction → over-gating on the
memory-bound trace. Evidence from `results/champsim.json`:

| trace | regime | unguarded_clean IPC | bouncer_clean IPC | clean_tax | bouncer_atk vs unguarded_atk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| perlbench | compute-bound | 1.929 | 1.897 | 1.7% | −1.4% |
| lbm | streaming (pollution) | 0.4585 | 0.448 | 2.3% | **+5.76% (CAP works)** |
| **roms** | **memory-bound** | **1.139** | **1.011** | **11.2%** | **−6.57%** |

- On **roms** the prefetcher genuinely helps IPC (`unguarded_clean 1.139` is the
  best of any cell), but the cache-hit proxy doesn't see that benefit, so Δ̂ reads
  low, the gate floors to π₀, and Bouncer pays an **11.2% clean tax**. Worse, under
  attack Bouncer's gating (`bouncer_attack 1.009`) even **loses to the attacked but
  ungated prefetcher** (`unguarded_attack 1.08`) — `−6.57%`. This is precisely
  over-gating: the audit fired because reward-Δ said "incompetent" while utility-Δ
  said "competent". The proxy mismatch, not the mechanism, caused the loss.
- On **lbm** the proxy and utility agree (pollution lowers both cache hits and
  IPC), so the floor CAP works as advertised (+5.76% over the attacked unguarded
  prefetcher). Where r tracks utility, Bouncer is sound.
- On **perlbench** prefetch barely matters, so all cells are within ~1.7% and the
  proxy mismatch is negligible.

The roms cell is the **clean empirical confirmation** of the N1 thesis: the
certificate is a statement about r, and roms is the case where `sign(Δ^reward) ≠
sign(Δ^utility)`. The paper already draws the correct conclusion — use the
controller's *own* in-silicon reward (Pythia accuracy × timeliness), which is
designed to track latency/MLP benefit, rather than a borrowed cache-hit signal
(champsim.json:53; bouncer.tex §8). roms is evidence that the limiting factor is
proxy fidelity, not the dueling/CUSUM/FSM machinery.

## 5. Honest verdict / recommended framing (no overclaim)

The claim is **defensible at its current, correctly-scoped level** and should NOT
be strengthened. Recommended one-line statement for the paper (matches
bouncer.tex:140-141 already):

> Bouncer certifies a bounded-regret floor *in the controller's reward metric*; its
> end-utility guarantee is exactly the fidelity of that reward as a proxy, and we
> demonstrate (roms) that a poorly-chosen proxy converts the floor into over-gating
> rather than into an unsafe miss — a conservative failure mode, but a real tax.

The conservative direction of the failure (over-gate, not under-gate) is worth
stating explicitly: with a *truthful-or-pessimistic* proxy Bouncer's errors cost
utility but never safety. The dangerous case — a proxy that *over*-credits C and
lets a utility-harming action through while reward looks fine — is NOT
demonstrated and is the open hole the §8 own-reward design must close. Do not
claim utility safety; claim reward-floor safety + conservative proxy behavior.

## Self-check / what a skeptic attacks
- "You're calling a tax a feature." — No: over-gating is presented as a real cost
  (11.2%) that *predicts* from the proxy thesis; the contribution is that the
  failure is conservative (safety-preserving) and attributable to r, not the
  mechanism.
- "roms could just be noise / tuning." — The operating point note (champsim.json:5-14)
  shows a looser (τ,H) was forced by noisier real Δ̂; a skeptic can argue the
  over-gate is a threshold-tuning artifact, not a proxy artifact. Mitigation: the
  sign of the effect (prefetch helps IPC but not cache-hits) is structural to
  memory-bound workloads, not threshold-dependent. Still, a clean isolation
  (same τ, swap only the reward signal cache-hit→own-reward) is NOT in the current
  results and would harden this to a proof rather than a strong inference.
