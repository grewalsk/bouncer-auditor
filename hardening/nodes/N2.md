# N2 — Per-domain audit budget: (δ_R, α_R), the coverage hole, and the feasibility bound

**Scope.** Derive the within-region resolvable competence gap δ_R and false-alarm
rate α_R as explicit functions of region size |R| and leader count, from the
set-dueling concentration bound (set_dueling.py:116-121, bouncer.tex:257-269).
Give the adversary's best-response region (smallest region the affordable leader
budget cannot separately audit — the "coverage hole") and state the feasibility
bound: leaders needed to audit a region of size |R| at resolution δ_R.

**Verdict: VIOLATION for the general mimicry-resistance / `C_secure` claim;
defended = 0 for that general property.** N2 is a *violation node*: its central
content is a closed-form derivation of the **coverage hole / slow-bleed attack**
(sec. 5) that *defeats* general mimicry resistance. Every piece of evidence below
supports the attack, not a general defense. The ONLY property positively supported
here is the narrow, *declared-region-with-intact-secret* case — which the paper
does not (and should not) advertise as general mimicry resistance.

To be explicit about why defended=0 and not 2:
* The set-dueling concentration bound (★) certifies separation **only on the union
  of audited domains, at resolution δ_R = k·σ_R**. It says nothing about
  sub-resolution regions or undeclared regions.
* (COVERAGE HOLE) below exhibits a redistributive adversary whose per-window harm
  sits *under* δ_R^min(B) — outside the union the safety-floor Lemma (lem:floor,
  bouncer.tex:320-339) is proven over — so the floor does **not** bound it.
* P4 (results/p4.json) empirically corroborates the *same* hole on the secret-leak
  axis: TPR collapses 1.0 → 0.075 (f=0.8) → 0 (f=1.0, latency → ∞). This is the
  attack succeeding, not a defense holding.
* N2 sec. 5 / caveat 3 state outright that the hole is "a real, un-closed
  vulnerability, not a solved case." A node that proves the attack cannot
  simultaneously defend the property the attack breaks.

The derivation itself (sec. 1–4) is correct, reproduces against the code, and
**strengthens the paper's qualitative "per-domain dueling costs more leaders per
protected domain" (bouncer.tex:790-792) into a closed-form budget** — but a sharper
budget for a *declared* domain is not a defense of *general* (any-region) security.
The honest defended level for `C_secure` is therefore **0**: no general defense is
established; only the narrow declared/intact-secret case, which N2 does not claim.

## 1. The single load-bearing identity

From `SetDueling.sigma_delta` (set_dueling.py:116-121) and bouncer.tex eq.(var)
(line 261), with per-decision reward in [0, r_max], m decisions/set/window, and
pool sizes n_L, n_F:

    Var(Δ̂) ≤ (r_max² / 4m) · (1/n_L + 1/n_F)              (★)

Everything below is (★) restricted to a region R and pushed through the Siegmund
CUSUM constants (cusum.py:95-116). **No new assumption is introduced** beyond the
ones already carrying the global guarantee (A1 bounded reward; A3-A5 secret,
partitionable, reseeded resource; within-R exchangeability up to bias β,
bouncer.tex:364-388).

### Per-domain instantiation
Audit region R with its *own* dueling pools drawn within R (the Prop. 6.2
construction, bouncer.tex:362-377). Let n_{L,R}, n_{F,R} be the Leader-L / Leader-F
counts **inside R**. Then directly from (★):

    σ_R²  :=  Var(Δ̂_R)  ≤  (r_max² / 4m) · (1/n_{L,R} + 1/n_{F,R})

If leaders are placed at the global areal density ρ = n_L/n_sets (the natural,
overhead-neutral allocation: every set carries the same 2-bit pool tag,
bouncer.tex:764), then n_{L,R} = n_{F,R} = ρ|R| and

    σ_R  ≤  (r_max / √(2m)) · 1/√(ρ|R|)   ∝  |R|^(−1/2).      (1)

**The region's audit noise scales as |R|^(−1/2).** Halving the region √2× the
noise. This is the entire story.

## 2. δ_R — within-region resolvable gap

Define δ_R as the smallest sustained competence gap the CUSUM can separate from
noise at the operating confidence. Using the CUSUM reference K = τ + γ/2
(cusum.py:49) and a k-σ separation rule (k≈3 for a ~3-σ in-control/out-of-control
gap; the gate needs E[Δ̂_R] to sit k·σ_R clear of K):

    δ_R  =  k · σ_R  =  k · r_max / √(2 m ρ |R|).             (2)

Numerically (r_max=1, m=64, ρ=1/8 from n_L=32, n_sets=256 — the paper's operating
point, bouncer.tex:456), 3-σ resolvable gaps, verified against set_dueling.py:

| \|R\| | n_{L,R} | σ_R | δ_R = 3σ_R |
|------|--------|------|-----------|
| 256 (whole resource) | 32 | 0.0156 | 0.047 |
| 128 | 16 | 0.0221 | 0.066 |
| 64  | 8  | 0.0312 | 0.094 |
| **32** | **4** | **0.0442** | **0.133** |
| 16  | 2  | 0.0625 | 0.188 |
| 8   | 1  | 0.0884 | 0.265 |

The |R|=32 row (n_{L,R}=4) **exactly reproduces the paper's measured "effective
in-region pool of ∼4 Leader-L sets" for the victim region in the headline P4
secrecy ablation** (bouncer.tex:571). This is the harness's strongest internal
self-consistency check on the derivation: the paper's empirical in-region pool is
the closed-form ρ|R| at the victim footprint, and its "few windows of latency"
follows from the inflated σ_R.

## 3. α_R — within-region per-window false alarm

α_R = 1/ARL0(δ standardized), with ARL0 from Siegmund (cusum.py:95-116):

    α_R = 1/ARL0,   ARL0 ≈ (e^(−2δb) + 2δb − 1)/(2δ²),
    δ = (K − E[Δ̂_R^clean]) / σ_R  (< 0 in-control),   b = H/σ_R + 1.166.   (3)

Because b ∝ 1/σ_R and δ ∝ 1/σ_R, ARL0 grows **exp(Θ(1/σ_R²)) = exp(Θ(ρ|R|))** —
i.e. false-alarm immunity is super-exponential in region size; equivalently, α_R
**blows up super-exponentially as |R| shrinks**. Verified (K=0.05, H=0.25,
E[Δ̂_clean]=0.10):

| \|R\| | n_{L,R} | σ_R | ARL0 | α_R / window |
|------|--------|------|------|--------------|
| 256 | 32 | 0.015625 | ~10^18 (clamped) | ~10^−18 |
| 64  | 8  | 0.031250 | 1.069×10^12 | 9.35×10^−13 |
| 32  | 4  | 0.044194 | 1.979×10^6  | 5.05×10^−7  |
| 16  | 2  | 0.062500 | 3.03×10^3  | 3.30×10^−4  |
| 8   | 1  | 0.088388 | 1.348×10^2  | 7.42×10^−3  |

> **Numeric-drift note (|R|=64 row).** Using the *exact* σ_R = r_max/√(2m·n_{L,R})
> = 1/√(2·64·8) = **0.03125** (not the 4-dp-rounded 0.0312), `cusum.siegmund_arl`
> returns ARL0 = **1.069×10^12**, α_R = **9.35×10^−13** — the values tabled here.
> Feeding the rounded σ=0.0312 instead returns 1.164×10^12 / 8.6×10^−13. The earlier
> 1.16e12/8.6e-13 figure was the rounded-input artifact; the exact-input values
> above are the correct ones and reproduce against set_dueling.py:116-121 +
> cusum.py:95-116. All other rows reproduce exactly.

So **(δ_R, α_R) jointly degrade as |R|^(−1/2) in resolution and exp(−Θ(|R|)) in
false-alarm headroom.** At a fixed CUSUM threshold H, shrinking the audited region
both coarsens δ_R and rapidly destroys the in-control margin until α_R is no longer
tolerable — the gate either goes blind (raise H to suppress α_R, losing δ_R) or
cries wolf (hold H, α_R explodes).

## 4. Feasibility bound — leaders to audit |R| at resolution δ_R

Invert (2) for the required in-region leader count (n_L=n_F case):

    n_{L,R}  ≥  k² r_max² / (2 m δ_R²).                       (FEAS)

This is **independent of |R|** — resolution δ_R fixes the *absolute* number of
leaders a region needs; |R| only sets whether that many sets physically exist in R
at the chosen density. Worked (k=3, m=64, r_max=1):

| target δ_R | n_{L,R} needed |
|-----------|----------------|
| 0.30 | 0.78 |
| 0.20 | 1.76 |
| 0.10 | 7.03 |
| 0.05 | 28.1 |

Total counter cost to protect a domain at resolution δ_R is 2·n_{L,R} (Leader-L +
Leader-F), reusing the existing ATD/sampler tag (bouncer.tex:774) — so the
overhead of per-domain auditing is **(FEAS) leaders × (number of protected
domains)**, the precise form of the paper's "more leader sets per protected
domain" (bouncer.tex:792).

## 5. The coverage hole (adversary best response)

Affordable in-region budget B (leaders the auditor will spend per domain). By
(FEAS), B leaders resolve gaps no finer than

    δ_R^min(B)  =  k r_max / √(2 m B).                        (4)

A redistributive adversary (bouncer.tex:390-393) that holds the *global* mean
fixed and concentrates harm in a region R is detectable **only if** the auditor
runs dueling pools inside R with enough leaders. The adversary's best response is
to choose the **smallest region whose in-region degradation gap stays under
δ_R^min(B)** — i.e. inflict a victim-local competence loss g such that

    (1 − f) · g  <  δ_R^min(B)  =  k r_max / √(2 m B),        (COVERAGE HOLE)

where f is the leaked-secret fraction sparing f·n_{L,R} leaders (bouncer.tex:
371-376). Two equivalent readings:

* **Fixed harm g, the adversary shrinks |R|.** Since placing B leaders in a region
  requires |R| ≥ B/ρ, any region smaller than ρ^{−1}·k²r_max²/(2m g²) physically
  cannot hold enough leaders at density ρ to resolve g. That region is the
  coverage hole: real degradation g lives there but Δ̂_R never clears H·σ_R.
* **Fixed region, the adversary shrinks harm.** Trickle g just below δ_R^min(B)
  and stay invisible indefinitely. This is the **slow-bleed** attack: per-window
  harm under the resolution floor, accumulated over the horizon. The safety-floor
  Lemma (bouncer.tex:320-330) does **not** save the victim here — the floor bounds
  regret vs π0 *in the reward metric on audited domains*; an unaudited (sub-
  resolution) region is outside the union over which the floor is proven.

**Empirical corroboration of the |R|→ small / f→1 collapse.** The P4 secrecy
ablation (results/p4.json) is exactly the n_{L,R}=4 region (|R|=32) being eaten as
f rises: TPR 1.0 at f≤0.4, 0.975 at f=0.6, **0.075 at f=0.8, 0 at f=1.0**, latency
1.2→∞ windows. Plugging f into (COVERAGE HOLE): the effective leader count is
(1−f)·4, so at f=0.8 only 0.8 leaders carry signal — below the n_{L,R}≈1 floor
where σ_R≈0.088 and α_R≈7×10^−3 already make the gate marginal. **f and |R| are
interchangeable axes of the same hole**: leaking 80% of a 4-leader region's secret
is operationally the same as auditing a 1-leader region with no leak. The paper
reports only the f-axis; (4) shows the |R|-axis is its twin, and this is the
sharper framing.

## 6. Honest limits / what NOT to overclaim

1. **δ_R = k·σ_R uses the variance *upper bound* (★), so δ_R is conservative
   (pessimistic) and α_R via Siegmund is an approximation** (cusum.py:18, the
   Brownian/overshoot 1.166 correction). The scaling laws |R|^(−1/2) and
   exp(−Θ(|R|)) are exact in the bound; the *constants* (k=3, the clean mean 0.10)
   are operating-point choices, not theorems. Do not present the numeric tables as
   measured FPRs — they are the analytical budget. (Consistent with the paper
   labelling its overhead/ARL numbers "analytical", bouncer.tex:756.)

2. **The heterogeneity bias β is additive on top of σ_R and does NOT shrink with
   |R|.** (★) governs sampling variance; β (hot/cold sets, conflict skew,
   bouncer.tex:394-396) is a *bias* floor on δ_R that secret reseeding shrinks but
   does not eliminate. So the true resolvable gap is δ_R ≈ k·σ_R + β, and for very
   small regions β can dominate. The derivation here bounds the *variance* term
   only; the bias term is a separate, un-quantified (β>0) limit the paper already
   flags. **This is the binding caveat: do not claim arbitrarily fine δ_R by
   spending leaders — β caps it.**

3. **The coverage hole is a real, un-closed vulnerability, not a solved case.** The
   feasibility bound says *how many leaders* close a hole of a given size; it does
   **not** say the auditor knows *where* to place them. Choosing R requires knowing
   the victim's footprint a priori. An adversary free to pick any sub-resolution
   region defeats a fixed per-domain allocation. The honest claim is: **Bouncer
   audits a *declared* domain at resolution (FEAS); it does not certify the absence
   of harm in *undeclared* sub-resolution regions.** This is strictly the §8
   own-reward / per-domain-coverage open problem, and connects to N1's open hole
   (over-crediting in an unaudited channel). Recommend the paper state (COVERAGE
   HOLE) explicitly rather than only the f→1 secrecy-leak axis.
